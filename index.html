🧺 Prepare

🙏 Puja steps

  1. Sit calmly and begin with a prayer to Lord Ganesha.
  2. Offer water, flowers, kumkum and akshata according to your custom.
  3. Pray to Goddess Lakshmi and light the diya safely.
  4. Chant a familiar mantra or sing your family’s Lakshmi/Ganesha aarti.
  5. Offer fruit or sweets as naivedya and share prasad.
  6. Worship books or account ledgers if this is part of your tradition.
  7. Conclude with aarti, gratitude and safe handling of lamps.

Lakshmi Puja · Traditional offerings

🪙 Gold & Silver Coins

Gold and silver coins are cherished by many families as symbols of prosperity, blessings, and gratitude. Use what suits your family tradition and budget; buying precious metals is optional.

🪷 How families use coins in puja

  1. Clean the coin gently and place it on a clean plate or cloth near the Lakshmi–Ganesha images.
  2. Offer flowers, akshat, or a prayer according to your household custom.
  3. Keep coins away from oil lamps, incense ash, water spills, and children who may swallow small objects.
  4. After puja, store them safely; share blessings through generosity and thoughtful use of wealth.

Explore two revered forms of Devi associated with protection, courage, and the victory of good over evil. Their stories and worship practices vary across regions and traditions.

🌺 Durga: meaning & devotion

  • Symbolism: strength, compassion, protection, and dharma.
  • Common symbols: lion or tiger, lotus, trident, and other divine weapons.
  • Celebrations: especially prominent during Navratri and Durga Puja; customs differ by community.

🔥 Chamunda: meaning & devotion

  • Symbolism: fierce protective energy and the overcoming of fear and destructive forces.
  • Depictions: iconography can be powerful or intense and differs across temples and historical traditions.
  • Worship: follow the customs of your family or temple; no single ritual is universal.

📚 The meaning of Diwali

Diwali is associated with the triumph of light over darkness and good over evil. Stories and emphasis differ among Hindu, Jain, Sikh, and other communities.

In many Hindu traditions, Lakshmi is honored for prosperity and well-being; in some regions, Diwali also marks Rama’s return to Ayodhya, while other communities observe distinct religious meanings.

💡 Family activity ideas

  • Make a rangoli together with flower petals.
  • Prepare a small diya-lighting area away from curtains.
  • Share sweets with neighbors or community helpers.
  • Tell children a family festival story.
  • Donate food or essentials to a local support group.
Care for people & planet

Safe and Eco-conscious Diwali

Enjoy the celebration while protecting children, elders, pets, and the environment.

🕯️

Diya safety

Place lamps on stable, heat-resistant surfaces, away from fabric and paper. Never leave an open flame unattended.

🌿

Lower-waste choices

Choose reusable decorations, natural flower rangoli, refillable lights, and minimal packaging. Sort waste after the celebration.

🎆

Firework awareness

Prefer quiet celebrations. Follow local laws and official safety guidance; keep children away from ignition areas and never relight a dud.

Keep emergency water or suitable fire equipment nearby, secure loose clothing and hair, and protect pets from noise. Follow local rules and current public-safety advisories.

Diwali has several regional and religious meanings. Broadly, it celebrates light, renewal, and the victory of good over evil, with distinct stories and practices across communities.

A simple sequence

🪔 Step-by-Step Diwali Puja

Use this as a flexible reminder, not a substitute for your family’s specific ritual instructions.

1. Prepare the space

Clean the altar, spread a clean red or yellow cloth if customary, and arrange the puja thali, water and offerings.

2. Place the deities and kalash

Arrange Lakshmi and Ganesha respectfully. Add a kalash, yantra or other sacred objects only as your tradition calls for.

3. Arrange flowers, diyas and offerings

Offer flowers, akshat, fruits, sweets and coins according to custom. Place lamps securely and keep matches or a lighter away from children.

4. Begin with prayer

Many households begin with Ganesha prayer, then worship Lakshmi. Recite your customary mantras or read a trusted puja booklet.

5. Perform aarti

Light the aarti lamp carefully, ring the bell if customary, and sing or play the aarti for the deities being worshipped.

6. Conclude respectfully

Offer prayers, distribute prasad, share blessings with family, and extinguish flames safely when finished.

Some households also worship Kubera, Saraswati or Vishnu on Diwali. The order and inclusion of these prayers differ; follow the sequence used in your home or temple.

🪷 Lakshmi–Ganesha Diya Placement

A simple visual reference for the main Diwali puja. Fuel, idol order and lamp placement vary by sampradaya, region and household; follow your family or temple tradition where it differs.

🪷

Goddess Lakshmi

Common choice: a clean diya filled with pure ghee is preferred by many households for Lakshmi Puja, symbolizing purity and auspiciousness. Sesame oil may also be used where customary; oil lamps are not universally excluded from Lakshmi worship.

Placement: set the lamp on a stable tray near the altar, without crowding the idol, flowers or cloth. Some household guides recommend a north-east, east or north-facing arrangement, but there is no single universal direction rule.

Ghee is a common preference
🐘

Lord Ganesha

Common choice: Ganesha is worshipped alongside Lakshmi during Diwali. A ghee diya is appropriate in many households; sesame or mustard oil may be used in other traditions. There is no universal rule that Ganesha must have an oil diya.

Placement: place the lamp safely near the altar, often in front or to one side as space allows. Keep the flame away from garlands, clothing, paper and the idols themselves.

Traditional lamp fuels

🌻 Cottonseed & Groundnut Oil Diyas

Learn about two oils used in some households. Their religious meanings and deity associations are regional customs, not universal Hindu rules; use the oil recommended by your family or temple.

🌿

Kapās Tel — Cottonseed Oil

Everyday use: may be used for household lamps where it is locally available and customary. Use clean, suitable lamp oil and a wick that fits the diya.

Traditional associations: some local practices connect oil lamps with Hanuman Ji or Shani Dev, but cottonseed oil is not a universal or required offering for either deity.

Direction and days: south or south-east directions and Tuesday or Saturday observances appear in some local guidance. Treat these as regional practices, not fixed rules.

Steadiness · DevotionLocal custom varies
🥜

Groundnut Oil — Peanut Oil

Everyday use: groundnut oil can be used for a diya if it is suitable for lamp use, burns reliably with the chosen wick and is part of your household practice. It is not automatically a ritual substitute for ghee in every puja.

Traditional associations: some families use it for daily prayer or local-deity worship. Specific links to Ganesha, Surya or prosperity are not universal and should be described as family or regional symbolism.

Direction and days: east or north-east may be recommended in some household guides, but there is no universal groundnut-oil direction rule.

Nourishment · Daily worshipFollow family tradition

🌼 Wick count and placement

  • One wick: commonly used for a simple daily lamp.
  • Two or more wicks: may have specific meanings in particular family or temple customs.
  • Altar: place lamps on a stable, heat-resistant tray with space from idols, cloth and decorations.
  • Entrance: welcoming diyas may be placed at the threshold if safe; keep walkways and exits clear.

Symbolic meanings assigned to wick counts are not universal rules.

🧭 Directions: common symbolic meanings

  • North-east: considered auspicious for worship in some Vastu-based guidance.
  • East: associated by some traditions with sunrise and new beginnings.
  • North: associated in some traditions with Kubera and prosperity.
  • South / west: may be specified for particular rites, deities or ancestral observances.

Prioritize fire safety and your household’s or temple’s instructions over a generalized direction chart.

🔥 Lamp safety: Never leave a diya unattended. Keep flames away from curtains, paper, hair, loose sleeves, children and pets. Use a stable holder, avoid placing lamps in walkways, and extinguish flames safely before leaving the room.
